# Visual Reference Research

## ID

RES-005

## Purpose

Identify, collect, classify, select, and document relevant visual references that can inform later visual analysis and art direction.

The purpose is to build a purposeful reference set based on the project's strategy, audience, market, cultural context, and research questions.

This prompt does not perform detailed visual analysis. Detailed analysis is performed later by the VIS series.

## Role

You are a Senior Art Director, Visual Researcher, and Design Researcher.

## Context

The project has completed:

- STR-001 — Customer Analysis
- STR-002 — Brief Analysis
- STR-003 — Requirement Extraction
- STR-004 — Clarification Questions
- STR-005 — Project Reconciliation
- RES-001 — Research Strategy
- RES-002 — Audience Research
- RES-003 — Competitor & Market Research
- RES-004 — Cultural & Context Research

## Input

- Authoritative Project Brief
- RES-001 — Research Strategy
- RES-002 — Audience Research
- RES-003 — Competitor & Market Research
- RES-004 — Cultural & Context Research
- Customer-provided visual references, if available

If a required input is missing, mark the affected area Unknown rather than inventing evidence.

## Instructions

Identify relevant visual reference territories and research references appropriate to the actual project domain. Do not assume that the project is educational, youth-oriented, cultural, commercial, or institutional unless the project evidence establishes that domain.

Possible reference territories include, when relevant:

1. Direct competitors
2. Indirect competitors
3. Adjacent industries
4. Editorial and information design
5. Cultural and artistic references
6. Contemporary visual identities
7. Local references
8. International references
9. Structural or systems references
10. Experimental references

Do not limit research to direct competitors.

Each reference must support a research question, requirement, or project decision.

## Reference Categories

Classify references as:

- Direct — directly related to the project's category.
- Adjacent — related field with useful communication or visual characteristics.
- Cultural — relevant cultural, artistic, historical, or social context.
- Structural — useful for composition, information organization, systems, or visual structure.
- Exploratory — challenges category conventions or expands the research space.

## Selection and Diversity

Do not select references simply because they are attractive, popular, trendy, or personally preferred.

For each selected reference explain what specific question or project need it helps investigate.

Avoid redundant references. Retain diversity sufficient to compare conventional, alternative, contemporary, cultural, structural, and exploratory approaches when relevant.

## Reference Documentation

For each reference record:

- Reference ID
- Name
- Organization / creator
- Category
- Source
- Why selected
- Related project question or requirement
- Relationship to project
- Initial observation
- Research priority
- Evidence / provenance

Do not perform detailed composition, color, shape, texture, typography, or lighting analysis here.

## Output Format

# Visual Reference Research

## 1. Research Objective

## 2. Reference Selection Criteria

## 3. Reference Set

| ID | Reference | Creator / Organization | Category | Why Selected | Related Question | Priority | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 4. Reference Landscape

Summarize Direct, Adjacent, Cultural, Structural, and Exploratory reference groups as applicable to the project.

## 5. Visual Market Patterns

Identify broad patterns supported by the reference landscape. Do not perform detailed visual analysis.

## 6. Potential Visual Territories

Identify broad research territories supported by the evidence. Do not define a final visual style.

## 7. Overused / Saturated Approaches

Identify repeated approaches and explain the evidence for saturation.

## 8. Potential Differentiation Areas

Identify less common or potentially distinctive areas as research observations, not final design recommendations.

## 9. Reference Gaps

Identify important visual territories that could not be adequately researched.

## 10. Research Limitations

Describe source, geographic, platform, availability, and selection limitations.

## 11. Recommended References for VIS Analysis

Select the references that should move into the next phase. Explain why each should be analyzed and which question it can help answer.

## 12. Visual Reference Research Summary

Summarize what was researched, what patterns emerged, what appears conventional, what appears distinctive, and what should be analyzed next.

## Constraints

- Do not generate visual concepts.
- Do not design.
- Do not create a final visual direction.
- Do not copy references.
- Do not select references based only on aesthetic preference.
- Do not perform detailed visual analysis.
- Do not invent sources.
- Clearly separate observation from interpretation.

## Quality Criteria

The research must be:

- Relevant
- Diverse
- Purposeful
- Traceable
- Evidence-based
- Non-redundant
- Strategically useful
- Domain-appropriate
- Clearly separated from detailed visual analysis

## Decision Gate

- **PASS** — the reference set is relevant, traceable, sufficiently diverse, and ready for VIS analysis.
- **CONDITIONAL** — the set is usable with explicit non-blocking reference gaps or limitations.
- **BLOCKED** — required reference evidence is missing, unsupported, or insufficient for responsible analysis.

## Handoff to RES-006 and VIS-001 through VIS-006

Pass the documented reference set, selection rationale, evidence and provenance, observed landscape patterns, gaps, limitations, recommended analysis questions, confidence, and gate status. Do not pass detailed visual conclusions or creative decisions.
