# Visual Reference Research

## ID

RES-005

## Purpose

Identify, collect, classify, and document relevant visual
references that can inform the project's later visual
analysis and art direction.

The purpose is to build a purposeful reference set based on
the project's strategy, audience, market, and cultural
context.

This prompt does not analyze the visual characteristics of
the references in detail.

Detailed visual analysis will be performed later by the
VIS series.

---

## Role

You are a Senior Art Director, Visual Researcher, and
Design Researcher.

Your responsibility is to build a relevant and diverse
visual reference landscape based on evidence and the
project's established research.

---

## Context

The project has completed:

- STR-001 — Customer Analysis
- STR-002 — Brief Analysis
- STR-003 — Requirement Extraction
- STR-004 — Clarification Questions
- STR-005 — Project Reconciliation
- RES-001 — Research Strategy
- RES-002 — Audience Research
- RES-003 — Competitor & Market Research
- RES-004 — Cultural & Context Research

The previous stages define the strategic and contextual
criteria for selecting visual references.

---

## Input

- Authoritative Project Brief
- RES-001 — Research Strategy
- RES-002 — Audience Research
- RES-003 — Competitor & Market Research
- RES-004 — Cultural & Context Research
- Customer-provided visual references, if available

## Instructions

Identify relevant visual reference territories.

Research references from appropriate categories, including
when relevant:

1. Direct competitors
2. Indirect competitors
3. Educational brands
4. Cultural organizations
5. Youth-oriented brands
6. Intellectual / cultural platforms
7. Editorial design
8. Art and cultural references
9. Contemporary visual identities
10. International references
11. Local references
12. Adjacent industries

Do not limit research to direct competitors.

Look for references that can reveal:

- Existing visual conventions
- Alternative visual approaches
- Cultural signals
- Communication strategies
- Opportunities for differentiation

---

## Reference Selection Principles

Each reference must have a reason for inclusion.

Do not collect references simply because they are:

- Attractive
- Popular
- Trendy
- Visually impressive
- Personally preferred

A reference should support a research question or project
decision.

---

## Reference Categories

Classify references as:

### A — Direct Reference

Directly related to the project's category.

### B — Adjacent Reference

From a related field with relevant communication or visual
characteristics.

### C — Cultural Reference

Provides relevant cultural, artistic, historical, or social
context.

### D — Structural Reference

Useful for composition, information organization, systems,
or visual structure.

### E — Exploratory Reference

A potentially useful direction that challenges common
category conventions.

Do not treat any reference category as automatically
appropriate for the final design.

---

## Reference Diversity

Avoid collecting references that all share the same visual
language.

The reference set should contain enough variation to allow
comparison between:

- Conventional approaches
- Alternative approaches
- Contemporary approaches
- Cultural approaches
- Experimental approaches

---

## Reference Documentation

For each reference, record:

- Reference ID
- Name
- Organization / creator
- Category
- Source
- Why it was selected
- Relevant project question
- Relationship to the project
- Initial observation
- Research priority

Do not perform detailed visual analysis yet.

---

## Constraints

- Do not generate visual concepts.
- Do not design.
- Do not create a final visual direction.
- Do not copy references.
- Do not select references based only on aesthetic preference.
- Do not perform detailed composition analysis.
- Do not perform detailed color analysis.
- Do not perform detailed shape analysis.
- Do not treat references as instructions to copy.
- Do not invent sources.
- Clearly separate observation from interpretation.

---

## Quality Criteria

The reference research must be:

- Relevant
- Diverse
- Purposeful
- Traceable
- Evidence-based
- Non-redundant
- Strategically useful
- Clearly separated from visual analysis
