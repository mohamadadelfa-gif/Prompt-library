### D-02 Competitor Set

- Raees Coffee — Tehran coffeehouse chain and roastery with vertically integrated coffee operations.

Market-reference set:

### D-03 Existing Identity Assets
Greenfield identity. No existing Noura logo, wordmark, typography system, packaging, signage, or brand-equity asset must be preserved.

### D-04 Meaning of “Distinctly Iranian”
The identity should feel Iranian through present-tense urban context rather than literal heritage symbolism. Acceptable territories include bilingual Persian/Latin typography, contemporary Iranian typographic rhythm, material cues, local urban texture, proportion, and abstract structural references. Avoid literal tile patterns, paisley, ornamental calligraphy imitation, ancient-emblem pastiche, Qajar nostalgia, and generic “Persian luxury.”

### D-05 Brand Story
Noura is a calm urban pause for curious people: coffee as a daily ritual for attention, conversation, and creative life in contemporary Tehran. This is a synthetic project decision, not external market evidence.

### D-06 Visual Reference Preference
Research should compare:
- direct Tehran coffee identities;
- contemporary Iranian bilingual typography/design practice;
- restrained editorial/information-design systems;
- tactile material systems that work across packaging and place.

No reference may be copied or treated as a final style decision.

### D-07 Production Constraints
- Persian and Latin must coexist cleanly.
- Core mark/system must survive one-color production.
- Packaging should be feasible with economical stocks, labels, stamps, or limited-color printing before premium finishes.
- Signage should work in painted, cut, or fabricated forms without depending on complex gradients or fine detail.
- Instagram and web components should derive from the same system rather than becoming separate visual identities.
- Small-size recognition remains mandatory.

### D-08 Budget Priority
Treat “medium budget” as a prioritization rule rather than an invented currency amount. Priority order:
1. core identity and bilingual typography;
2. storefront/signage;
3. cups and packaging;
4. menu and Instagram system;
5. website visual layer.

# RES-003 — Competitor & Market Research

## Direct Competitors

### Sam Coffee Roasters
First-party company description emphasizes quality across the whole experience, deliberate design, authenticity that does not mean being old, and future orientation. A published identity case by Studio Kargah documents dedicated identity work for Sam Café and related Sam extensions.

Relevance to Noura: Sam occupies a design-aware, quality-led territory close to Noura’s desired creative audience, so generic claims of intentionality, quality, or contemporary authenticity may not create sufficient differentiation by themselves.

### Raees Coffee
Raees Group’s first-party site describes a Tehran-based coffeehouse chain with 12 branches and a supply-chain model spanning sourcing, production, distribution, roastery, academy, and cafés. Its self-presentation emphasizes coffee quality, expertise, freshness, and education.

Relevance to Noura: competing on “serious coffee expertise” alone would enter a territory already strongly articulated by an established operator.

## Market Reference

### Lamiz Coffee
Lamiz remains a significant visual/category reference for Tehran coffee branding and has used prominent bilingual signage and a recognizable Latin-letter mark. Because multiple 2026 reports describe judicial closure of Tehran branches and current operational status is not reliably established in this run, Lamiz is treated as a historical/current-market reference rather than a confirmed active direct competitor.

## Market Patterns
- Quality and expertise are established claims in the category.
- Intentional design/contemporary authenticity are also visible competitor positions.
- Bilingual public-facing identity is already present in Tehran coffee branding.

## Potential Differentiation Hypotheses
- A calmer, editorial, present-tense Iranian identity could be differentiated if it avoids both generic global specialty-coffee minimalism and literal heritage styling.
- Noura may gain distinction by making the Persian/Latin relationship structurally central rather than merely placing two scripts beside each other.

These are hypotheses for VIS/art-direction evaluation, not market facts.

## Gate
`CONDITIONAL`

Reason: the focused competitor sample is sufficient for a synthetic visual-strategy run but not for a comprehensive market study.

# RES-004 — Cultural & Context Research

## Key Findings
- Peer-reviewed 2026 research characterizes central Tehran café culture through local/global hybridization and “glocal” cultural practice.
- This supports investigating contemporary Iranian identity as a negotiated present-tense condition rather than forcing a binary between “traditional Persian” and “international modern.”
- Studio Melli, a contemporary Iranian-rooted graphic/type practice, explicitly describes multilingual typography as central to carrying Persian visual culture into contemporary dialogue. This is a practitioner reference, not proof of audience preference.
- Recent public controversy around Lamiz cup graphics demonstrates that symbolic interpretation of public visual material can have unusually high contextual stakes in Tehran. Therefore accidental political symbolism should be included in cultural-risk review; this does not mean design should become politically expressive or politically neutral by default.

## Cultural Opportunities
- Bilingual typography can be treated as a genuine design system rather than decorative translation.
- Local materiality, urban texture, language, rhythm, and proportion can communicate cultural situatedness without literal historic motifs.

## Cultural Risks
- Orientalist/self-exoticizing “Persian” decoration.
- Token Persian script attached to an otherwise generic imported identity.
- Nostalgia that contradicts the supplied contemporary positioning.
- Unintended symbolic associations in highly visible public graphics.

`PASS`

# RES-006 — Research Synthesis

## Executive Summary
Noura operates in a Tehran café context where social experience, design, cultural belonging, and global/local aesthetics can intersect. Direct competitors already claim quality, expertise, intentionality, and contemporary authenticity. The evidence therefore supports investigating a more specific identity territory: a calm, bilingual, editorial system rooted in present-tense Tehran through language, materiality, and structure rather than stereotypical heritage motifs. This is a research opportunity, not yet the final visual direction.

## Strategic Tensions

| ID | Tension | Side A | Side B | VIS Question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T-01 | Local specificity vs global contemporary legibility | Iranian cultural connection is required. | Traditional/stereotypical Persian styling is prohibited. | Which non-literal visual signals can create local specificity? |
| T-02 | Calm minimalism vs category sameness | Minimal/editorial restraint is preferred. | Minimal specialty-coffee branding is easy to make generic. | What structural signature can make restraint recognizable? |
| T-03 | Strong typography vs bilingual imbalance | Typography is a preferred core asset. | Persian and Latin can become visually unequal or tokenistic. | What hierarchy makes both scripts feel intentional? |
| T-04 | Tactility vs medium-budget scalability | Tactile materiality is desired. | The system must remain economical and scalable. | Which tactile effects survive simple stocks, labels, stamps, paint, and one-color production? |

## Recommended VIS Analysis
1. `VR-01` Sam Café — identify system strengths, repeated devices, and imitation risks.
2. `VR-02` Raees — identify authority/quality signals and category conventions.
3. `VR-03` Lamiz — identify recognition mechanisms and bilingual-signage conventions, with status caveat.
4. `VR-04` Studio Melli — study bilingual typographic relationships as cultural/structural evidence, not a style to copy.
5. Compare these against Noura’s mandatory anti-stereotype, small-size, cross-media, and muted/editorial constraints.

## Remaining Unknowns
- Exact store architecture and neighborhood.
- Actual production suppliers and fabrication capabilities.
- Noura-specific audience validation.
- Final creative direction, palette values, typefaces, logo form, image language, layout grammar, and material specification; these remain deliberately undecided for the VIS/VDNA/ART stages.
