# English Beyond Language — Content & Communication Rules

## Status

Project: English Beyond Language

Authority: Human-approved project rules

These rules apply to EBL social-media content, visual design, art direction, generation, and QC. They are project-specific and must not be promoted automatically into global system rules.

Where these rules conflict with an older EBL assumption, the newer human instruction in this file takes priority.

---

## EBL-RULE-014 — No Elitist Positioning

English Beyond Language must not present itself, its teacher, its students, its cultural references, or its educational method through elitist positioning.

Avoid communication that implies:

- intelligent or cultured people are superior to others;
- EBL is only for a socially or intellectually exclusive group;
- students must already possess high cultural capital to belong;
- difficulty, obscurity, academic language, or cultural references are valuable merely because they exclude people;
- learning English is a marker of class superiority or personal worth.

Prefer:

- curiosity over superiority;
- invitation over exclusion;
- intellectual openness over intellectual status;
- learning as participation and growth;
- cultural material explained accessibly;
- respect for different starting points.

EBL may be thoughtful, cultural, artistic, and intellectually serious without becoming elitist.

## EBL-RULE-015 — Motivational Social Communication

Social-media communication should motivate students to participate, learn, think, speak, and continue developing their English.

Motivation should feel:

- encouraging;
- achievable;
- respectful;
- curious;
- active;
- learner-centered.

Prefer motivation based on:

- progress;
- participation;
- discovery;
- practical ability;
- confidence through practice;
- understanding books, films, art, culture, and ideas;
- being able to express a personal view.

Avoid:

- empty motivational slogans;
- exaggerated promises;
- shame-based motivation;
- fear of being left behind;
- superiority-based motivation;
- unrealistic transformation claims.

A useful EBL motivational pattern is:

```text
RELATABLE SITUATION
→ ACHIEVABLE POSSIBILITY
→ SMALL INTELLECTUAL / LANGUAGE CHALLENGE
→ INVITATION TO PARTICIPATE
```

## EBL-RULE-016 — No Random Content or Random Visual Design

EBL must not produce random content, random topics, or random visual decisions merely to fill a posting schedule.

Every content item must have a reason to exist.

Before producing a post, carousel, Story, Reel, or visual asset, establish:

```text
PROJECT GOAL
→ AUDIENCE NEED / INTEREST
→ CONTENT PURPOSE
→ MESSAGE
→ FORMAT FUNCTION
→ VISUAL CONCEPT
→ TYPOGRAPHIC / COMPOSITIONAL DECISIONS
```

Content should connect to one or more EBL purposes such as:

- language learning;
- independent participation;
- speaking and expressing ideas;
- cultural discussion;
- books;
- films;
- art;
- contemporary or relevant ideas;
- learner motivation;
- demonstrating the EBL learning method;
- consultation / class inquiry / registration when appropriate.

Visual elements must also have a function.

Do not add a shape, color field, texture, illustration, image, typographic treatment, or compositional device merely because it looks interesting.

Ask:

```text
WHAT DOES THIS ELEMENT COMMUNICATE?
WHY IS IT HERE?
HOW DOES IT SUPPORT THIS CONTENT?
```

If there is no meaningful answer, remove or reconsider it.

## EBL-RULE-017 — B1 Content-Language Standard

Public-facing EBL social-media content should normally be written at approximately **CEFR B1 English level**.

This rule governs the language used to communicate the content, not the intellectual value of the subject.

EBL can discuss books, films, art, culture, society, and complex ideas, but the explanation should remain accessible to a B1 learner.

### B1 writing behavior

Prefer:

- common, useful vocabulary;
- clear sentence structures;
- mostly short or medium-length sentences;
- one main idea per sentence when possible;
- explicit connections between ideas;
- concrete examples;
- direct questions;
- familiar verbs and phrasal verbs where appropriate;
- brief explanation of unavoidable advanced terms.

Avoid by default:

- dense academic prose;
- unnecessary jargon;
- long nested clauses;
- abstract noun-heavy sentences;
- obscure synonyms when a common word works;
- idioms that are difficult to infer without context;
- complex wording used to sound sophisticated.

### Important distinction

```text
B1 LANGUAGE
≠
SIMPLE-MINDED CONTENT
```

The idea may be challenging. The language used to explain it should be accessible.

### Supersession note

This rule supersedes any earlier EBL wording that implies public social-media copy should default to B2+ linguistic complexity.

The audience may develop beyond B1, and specific advanced-learning content may intentionally introduce higher-level vocabulary, but the default explanatory language remains B1 unless the human explicitly requests another level.

# Combined Content Gate

Before approving EBL social-media content, verify all four questions:

```text
1. ELITISM CHECK
Does the content invite learners without implying cultural/intellectual superiority?

2. MOTIVATION CHECK
Does it encourage a realistic next step, participation, curiosity, or progress?

3. PURPOSE CHECK
Can we explain why this content and its visual decisions exist for EBL?

4. B1 LANGUAGE CHECK
Can a B1 learner understand the main message without unnecessary linguistic difficulty?
```

If any answer is `NO`, revise before production or publication.
