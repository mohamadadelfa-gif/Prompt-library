# Named Style Study Protocol

## Purpose

When a project explicitly names an artist, movement, school, historical visual language, or other recognizable style source, study that named style before synthesizing the supplied references.

The purpose is not to imitate the source. The purpose is to understand the underlying visual logic deeply enough to make an original, responsible translation for the client's communication problem.

## Position in Workflow

```text
NAMED STYLE
   ↓
STYLE STUDY
   ↓
SUPPLIED REFERENCE ANALYSIS
   ↓
REFERENCE STYLE SYNTHESIS
   ↓
CLIENT / BRAND TRANSLATION
   ↓
PLATFORM TRANSLATION
```

A named style study is required whenever the brief contains a specific artist, movement, school, or stylistic attribution that materially affects the visual direction.

## Source Discipline

Separate all findings into:

- SOURCE — documented information about the artist/style from credible primary or authoritative sources.
- OBSERVATION — directly observed characteristics in supplied artworks/references.
- INTERPRETATION — analytical conclusions about formal behavior or perceptual effect.
- TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE — a deliberate design abstraction approved for project use.

Do not present interpretation as historical fact.

## Study Dimensions

### 1. Context and Intent

Understand the artist's relevant period, working context, stated interests, and artistic questions only as far as they help explain the visual language.

### 2. Line / Gesture

Study how line behaves:

- weight
- rhythm
- speed / hesitation
- continuity
- contour
- wandering / directional movement
- relationship to form
- visible human gesture

### 3. Shape / Form

Study:

- geometric vs organic forms
- simplification
- deformation
- scale relationships
- natural forms becoming signs
- repetition
- fragmentation

### 4. Color

Study:

- palette families
- temperature
- contrast
- saturation
- color relationships
- color as structure vs decoration
- perceived emotional effect

### 5. Material / Brush / Surface

Study:

- brush behavior
- pigment deposition
- edge quality
- transparency / opacity
- layering
- paper or support interaction
- texture
- signs of hand application

### 6. Composition / Space

Study:

- asymmetry
- visual weight
- negative space
- balance
- rhythm
- overlap
- edge tension
- focal movement
- order vs freedom

### 7. Symbol / Sign System

Identify recurring symbolic behavior and determine whether forms act as literal objects, visual signs, metaphors, or compositional devices.

### 8. Process / Construction Logic

Ask how the visual result appears to be constructed, not only what it looks like.

For example:

```text
gesture → shape → color → overlap → correction → final tension
```

The process logic is often more transferable than the final surface.

### 9. Perceptual / Emotional Effect

Mandatory question:

> What does the viewer feel, and which formal decisions create that feeling?

Describe emotional effects carefully as interpretation, for example:

- playful
- curious
- intimate
- exploratory
- poetic
- warm
- human
- intellectually playful
- calm
- mysterious

These are hypotheses unless directly supported by the brief or research.

### 10. Communication Effect

Determine how the style changes attention and meaning:

- invites closer looking
- slows attention
- increases curiosity
- humanizes information
- creates cultural/intellectual depth
- makes complex information feel approachable
- creates distinction from generic visual categories

## Paul Klee Example — Study Focus

For a Paul Klee reference, do not reduce the study to “geometric shapes + muted colors.” Investigate the relationships among line, movement, color, signs, geometry, natural forms, material surface, and the balance between structure and spontaneity.

The project-level interpretation should investigate whether the supplied references create a feeling that is:

- curious
- human
- exploratory
- playful but intelligent
- warm
- thoughtful
- culturally aware

These qualities must be treated as interpreted perceptual effects, not as universal facts about every work by Klee.

## Non-Task

The study must not:

- copy a specific artwork;
- reproduce an artist's exact composition;
- declare a client brand to be identical to the artist;
- turn interpretation into historical fact;
- generate the final Instagram template.

## Decision Gate

PASS — the named style is understood sufficiently to inform reference analysis and translation.

CONDITIONAL — usable with explicit uncertainty or limited evidence.

BLOCKED — insufficient reliable understanding or contradictory evidence prevents responsible translation.
