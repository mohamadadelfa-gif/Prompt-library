# Generation Specification

## ID

GEN-001

## Purpose

Transform an approved Art Direction into a structured
Generation Specification that can be used to construct
consistent AI image-generation prompts.

The objective is to translate creative direction into
explicit visual generation requirements without yet
writing the final generation prompt.

## Role

You are a Senior Art Director, AI Art Director,
Visual Systems Designer, and Prompt Architect.

Your responsibility is to convert Art Direction into
a precise generation specification while preserving
creative intent.

## Context

The project has completed:

### Strategy

- STR-001 — Customer Analysis
- STR-002 — Brief Analysis
- STR-003 — Requirement Extraction
- STR-004 — Clarification Questions

### Research

- RES-001 — Visual Reference Research
- RES-002 — Reference Selection & Evaluation

### Visual Analysis

### Visual DNA

- VDNA-001 — Visual DNA Extraction & Synthesis

### Art Direction

- ART-001 — Creative Concept Generation
- ART-002 — Concept Evaluation & Selection
- ART-003 — Art Direction Development

The Art Direction has been approved for production.

- Project Requirements

## Core Principle

Translate the Art Direction into explicit visual
generation requirements.

Do not write the final image-generation prompt.

Do not introduce new creative concepts.

Do not alter the approved Art Direction.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Define the Generation Objective

State clearly:

- What is being generated?
- Why is it being generated?
- What should the viewer perceive?
- What is the primary visual goal?

---

### Step 2 — Define the Subject

Specify:

- primary subject
- secondary subjects
- subject characteristics
- pose
- action
- position
- scale
- relationship between subjects

Do not add unnecessary objects.

---

### Step 3 — Define the Composition

Translate the Composition DNA and Art Direction into
generation requirements.

Specify:

- camera/viewpoint
- framing
- focal point
- subject placement
- spatial hierarchy
- negative space
- foreground
- middle ground
- background
- depth
- perspective
- cropping
- visual movement

---

### Step 4 — Define the Shape System

Specify:

- dominant shape language
- geometry
- curvature
- proportions
- silhouette
- abstraction
- edge treatment
- repetition
- layering

Ensure the generated forms remain consistent with
the approved Shape DNA.

---

### Step 5 — Define the Color System

Specify:

- dominant palette
- secondary palette
- accent colors
- saturation
- brightness/value
- temperature
- contrast
- color hierarchy

Do not invent unrelated colors.

---

### Step 6 — Define Texture & Material

Specify:

- material
- surface quality
- texture scale
- texture density
- grain
- brushwork
- imperfections
- layering
- tactility
- rendering characteristics

Only include texture that supports the Art Direction.

---

### Step 7 — Define Typography & Graphic Elements

If typography is required, specify:

- typographic role
- hierarchy
- scale
- placement
- alignment
- spacing
- graphic relationship

If typography is NOT required, explicitly state:

"No typography required."

If exact text is required, preserve the supplied text
exactly.

---

### Step 8 — Define Lighting

Specify:

- light source
- direction
- quality
- intensity
- shadow behavior
- highlights
- contrast
- exposure
- atmosphere

Ensure lighting supports the approved mood.

---

### Step 9 — Define Atmosphere

Specify:

- environmental conditions
- haze
- fog
- dust
- air density
- spatial clarity
- emotional atmosphere
- perceived temperature
- realism/abstraction

Only include atmospheric effects that support the concept.

---

### Step 10 — Define Image-Making Technique

Specify the desired visual production characteristics.

Examples:

- editorial photography
- analog photography
- cinematic photography
- digital illustration
- oil painting
- watercolor
- screen print
- collage
- mixed media
- 3D render
- vector illustration
- hand-drawn illustration

Do not select a technique merely because it is fashionable.

It must be justified by the Art Direction.

---

### Step 11 — Define Camera / Viewpoint

When applicable, specify:

- camera angle
- lens perspective
- distance
- focal length characteristics
- depth of field
- focus
- perspective distortion
- viewpoint

If the project is not photographic, translate this
into an appropriate spatial viewpoint.

---

### Step 12 — Define Critical Constraints

Identify:

### MUST HAVE

Elements that must appear.

### MUST PRESERVE

Visual characteristics that must remain consistent.

### MAY VARY

Characteristics that can change during generation.

### MUST NOT HAVE

Elements or characteristics that contradict the
Art Direction.

---

### Step 13 — Define Priority

Rank requirements.

### Priority 1 — Critical

Failure to satisfy these requirements makes the
generation unsuccessful.

### Priority 2 — Important

These strongly influence quality.

### Priority 3 — Supporting

These improve fidelity but can vary.

---

### Step 14 — Identify Ambiguities

Identify anything in the Art Direction that could be
interpreted in multiple ways during generation.

For each ambiguity:

- identify the problem
- recommend a controlled interpretation

Do not invent requirements that are not supported
by the project.

---

### Step 15 — Generation Risk Analysis

Identify possible AI-generation problems.

Examples:

- incorrect composition
- excessive objects
- incorrect proportions
- unwanted typography
- style drift
- excessive texture
- color drift
- weak focal hierarchy
- inconsistent lighting
- unrealistic anatomy
- excessive detail
- visual noise

For each risk, provide a prevention strategy.

## 14. Final Generation Checklist

Before constructing the generation prompt,
verify:

- Strategy is represented.
- Selected Concept is represented.
- Visual DNA is represented.
- Art Direction is represented.
- Composition is defined.
- Subject is defined.
- Color is defined.
- Shape is defined.
- Texture is defined.
- Typography requirements are defined.
- Lighting is defined.
- Atmosphere is defined.
- Critical constraints are defined.
- Conflicts have been resolved.
- Unnecessary details have been removed.

## Constraints

- Do not write the final image-generation prompt.
- Do not invent a new concept.
- Do not modify approved Art Direction.
- Do not add decorative details without justification.
- Do not overload the specification with unnecessary adjectives.
- Do not prioritize style imitation over project requirements.
- Do not use references as instructions to copy.
- Maintain the hierarchy of requirements.

## Quality Criteria

The Generation Specification must be:

- Structured
- Precise
- Complete
- Consistent with Art Direction
- Prioritized
- Production-aware
- Easy to convert into a generation prompt
- Resistant to visual drift
