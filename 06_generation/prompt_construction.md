## Core Principle

The final generation prompt must be a translation,
not a reinvention.

Do not introduce new creative ideas.

Do not weaken the hierarchy of the Generation
Specification.

Do not turn the specification into unnecessary
descriptive prose.

Every major phrase in the final prompt should serve
a visual purpose.

# Prompt Construction Process

## Step 1 — Identify the Primary Objective

Determine the single most important visual objective.

The beginning of the prompt should establish:

- subject
- action or state
- conceptual purpose
- primary visual focus

Do not begin with secondary details.

---

## Step 2 — Establish Composition

Translate the composition requirements into concise
visual instructions.

Include relevant elements such as:

- viewpoint
- framing
- subject placement
- focal hierarchy
- negative space
- foreground
- middle ground
- background
- depth
- perspective
- cropping

Do not include every possible camera term.

Use only those that materially affect the intended image.

---

## Step 3 — Establish Form

Translate Shape & Form requirements.

Include:

- shape language
- geometry
- organic forms
- proportions
- silhouettes
- edges
- abstraction
- repetition
- layering

Preserve the established visual vocabulary.

---

## Step 4 — Establish Color

Translate the Color Specification.

Prioritize:

1. dominant palette
2. secondary palette
3. accent strategy
4. saturation
5. value
6. temperature
7. contrast

Do not add colors that are not supported by the
Generation Specification.

---

## Step 5 — Establish Material & Texture

Describe the relevant material and surface behavior.

Include:

- material
- surface
- grain
- brushwork
- texture density
- imperfections
- layering
- tactile qualities

Avoid stacking synonymous texture adjectives.

For example, do not write:

"rough, gritty, coarse, rugged, grainy texture"

when one precise description is sufficient.

---

## Step 6 — Establish Lighting

Translate the Lighting Specification.

Include:

- light direction
- quality
- intensity
- shadow behavior
- highlights
- contrast
- exposure

Lighting should reinforce the composition and mood.

---

## Step 7 — Establish Atmosphere

Translate the Atmosphere Specification.

Include only relevant characteristics such as:

- haze
- fog
- dust
- environmental depth
- air density
- spatial clarity
- mood

Do not add atmospheric effects simply to make the
prompt sound cinematic.

---

## Step 8 — Establish Image-Making Method

Describe the intended medium or visual production method.

Examples:

- editorial photography
- analog photography
- hand-painted illustration
- mixed-media collage
- screen print
- 3D render
- vector illustration

Only use a medium supported by the Art Direction.

---

## Step 9 — Typography

If typography is required:

- preserve exact supplied text
- specify placement
- specify hierarchy
- specify scale
- specify alignment
- specify relationship to imagery

If typography is not required:

Do not add text.

---

## Step 10 — Apply Constraints

Translate:

### MUST HAVE

into explicit requirements.

Translate:

### MUST NOT HAVE

into negative constraints when appropriate.

Do not create a massive negative prompt containing
irrelevant objects.

Negative constraints should target actual risks.

---

# Prompt Hierarchy

Construct the prompt in this order unless the target
model requires another structure:

1. Primary subject
2. Concept / visual purpose
3. Composition
4. Form
5. Color
6. Material / texture
7. Lighting
8. Atmosphere
9. Typography / graphics
10. Image-making characteristics
11. Critical constraints

The hierarchy should reflect the importance of the
visual requirements.

---

# Prompt Language Rules

Use:

- concrete visual language
- concise descriptions
- observable characteristics
- specific relationships
- controlled adjectives
- spatial language
- material language
- lighting language

Avoid:

- vague adjectives
- excessive metaphors
- contradictory instructions
- unnecessary repetition
- marketing language
- unexplained technical jargon
- generic words such as "beautiful", "amazing",
  "stunning", or "professional"

Instead of:

"Make it beautiful and cinematic."

Prefer:

"Controlled low-key lighting with soft directional
illumination, deep but detailed shadows, restrained
highlights, and atmospheric spatial separation."

---

# Model Adaptation

The core prompt must remain model-agnostic.

After creating the core prompt, determine whether
the target generation system requires adaptation.

Separate:

### CORE PROMPT

The universal visual instruction.

### MODEL ADAPTATION

Model-specific formatting or syntax.

Never allow model-specific syntax to alter the
underlying Art Direction.

# Prompt Validation

Before finalizing, check:

## Strategy

Does the prompt serve the project objective?

## Concept

Does the prompt preserve the selected concept?

## Visual DNA

Does the prompt preserve the Visual DNA?

## Art Direction

Does the prompt follow the Art Direction?

## Generation Specification

Are all Priority 1 requirements represented?

## Composition

Is the visual hierarchy clear?

## Color

Is the color system preserved?

## Shape

Is the shape language preserved?

## Texture

Is texture controlled?

## Lighting

Does lighting support the intended mood?

## Typography

Is text handled correctly?

## Constraints

Are major risks addressed?

## Contradictions

Does the prompt contain conflicting instructions?

## Prompt Density

Is the prompt unnecessarily long?

Remove information that does not materially improve
generation quality.

---

# Final Quality Test

The final prompt should satisfy:

- clear primary subject
- clear composition
- clear visual hierarchy
- coherent color system
- consistent shape language
- controlled texture
- appropriate lighting
- appropriate atmosphere
- correct typography behavior
- no unnecessary creative invention
- no contradiction with Visual DNA
- no contradiction with Art Direction

## Constraints

- Do not invent concepts.
- Do not alter the selected concept.
- Do not modify Visual DNA.
- Do not add unsupported visual styles.
- Do not imitate references literally.
- Do not add unnecessary adjectives.
- Do not create contradictory instructions.
- Do not prioritize prompt length over clarity.
- Do not assume model-specific syntax unless the target
  model is explicitly identified.
- Do not create final images.
