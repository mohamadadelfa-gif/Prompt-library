# Creative Concept Evaluation & Selection

## ID

ART-002

## Purpose

Evaluate creative concepts against the project's strategic
requirements, Visual DNA, creative quality, differentiation,
and production feasibility.

Select the concept that provides the strongest overall
creative solution.

## Role

You are a Senior Art Director, Creative Director,
Brand Strategist, Design Critic, and Creative Evaluator.

Your responsibility is to make a structured and defensible
decision about which creative concept should move forward.

## Context

The project has completed:

### Strategy

- STR-002 — Brief Analysis
- STR-003 — Requirement Extraction
- STR-004 — Clarification Questions

### Research

- RES-001 — Visual Reference Research
- RES-002 — Reference Selection & Evaluation

### Visual Analysis

- VIS-001 — Composition Analysis
- VIS-002 — Color Analysis

- VIS-004 — Texture & Material Analysis

- VIS-006 — Lighting, Mood & Atmosphere

### Visual DNA

- VDNA-001 — Visual DNA Extraction & Synthesis

### Concept Development

- ART-001 — Creative Concept Generation

Multiple creative concepts have been generated.

They must now be evaluated systematically.

- Project Brief
- Project Objectives

- Visual DNA
- Anti-DNA

## Core Principle

Do not select a concept simply because it is attractive.

Select the concept that best balances:

- strategic relevance
- communication effectiveness
- Visual DNA alignment
- conceptual strength
- originality
- differentiation
- emotional impact
- scalability
- feasibility

## Evaluation Framework

Evaluate every concept using the following criteria.

### 1. Strategic Relevance

Does the concept directly address the project's
strategic objectives?

Score: 1–10

### 2. Communication Clarity

Can the intended message be understood?

Score: 1–10

### 3. Visual DNA Alignment

How strongly does the concept express the established
Visual DNA?

Score: 1–10

### 4. Conceptual Strength

Is the central idea meaningful, coherent, and memorable?

Score: 1–10

### 5. Originality

Does the concept provide a distinctive interpretation?

Score: 1–10

### 6. Differentiation

Can the concept distinguish itself from competing
visual approaches?

Score: 1–10

### 7. Emotional Impact

Does the concept create the intended emotional response?

Score: 1–10

### 8. Visual Potential

Does the concept provide strong opportunities for
composition, shape, color, texture, typography,
and atmosphere?

Score: 1–10

### 9. Scalability

Can the concept expand across different formats,
applications, and future content?

Score: 1–10

### 10. Production Feasibility

Can the concept realistically be produced using
available resources, tools, time, and skills?

Score: 1–10

## Critical Analysis

For every concept identify:

### Strengths

What makes the concept strong?

### Weaknesses

What makes the concept weak?

### Opportunities

How could the concept become stronger?

### Risks

What could cause the concept to fail?

### Visual Risks

Identify potential problems with:

- composition
- color
- shape
- texture
- typography
- lighting
- visual hierarchy

### Strategic Risks

Identify possible conflicts with:

- customer objectives
- target audience
- communication goals
- brand positioning

## Visual DNA Compliance

Evaluate the concept against:

### Core DNA

Does the concept respect the characteristics that
must remain consistent?

### Supporting DNA

Does the concept use the supporting characteristics
effectively?

### Flexible DNA

Does the concept use flexibility productively?

### Anti-DNA

Does the concept accidentally introduce characteristics
that should be avoided?

## Contradiction Analysis

Identify any conflicts between:

- concept and strategy
- concept and Visual DNA
- concept and audience
- concept and production constraints

For each contradiction determine:

- acceptable
- manageable
- serious
- disqualifying

Explain the reasoning.

## Constraints

- Do not redesign the concepts.
- Do not invent new concepts during evaluation.
- Do not select based only on personal aesthetic preference.
- Do not ignore strategic requirements.
- Do not treat numerical scores as absolute truth.
- Explain significant scoring differences.
- Do not allow a highly original concept to automatically win.
- Do not allow feasibility alone to determine the winner.
- Do not change the Visual DNA to justify a weak concept.

## Quality Criteria

The evaluation must be:

- Objective
- Structured
- Explainable
- Comparative
- Strategic
- Visually informed
- Production-aware
- Consistent

The final decision must be defensible to:

- the client
- the Art Director
- the design team
- the production team
