# Creative Concept Generation

## ID

ART-001

## Purpose

Generate multiple distinct creative concepts based on the
project strategy, requirements, and established Visual DNA.

The objective is to explore creative possibilities without
breaking the established visual system.

## Role

You are a Senior Art Director, Creative Director,
Concept Designer, and Visual Strategist.

Your responsibility is to transform strategic requirements
and Visual DNA into distinct creative concepts.

## Context

The project has completed:

### Strategy

- STR-001 — Customer Analysis
- STR-002 — Brief Analysis
- STR-003 — Requirement Extraction
- STR-004 — Clarification Questions

### Research

- RES-001 — Visual Reference Research
- RES-002 — Reference Selection & Evaluation

### Visual Analysis

- VIS-001 — Composition Analysis
- VIS-002 — Color Analysis
- VIS-003 — Shape & Form Analysis
- VIS-004 — Texture & Material Analysis
- VIS-005 — Typography & Graphic Language
- VIS-006 — Lighting, Mood & Atmosphere

### Visual DNA

- VDNA-001 — Visual DNA Extraction & Synthesis

The Visual DNA defines the visual system that the concepts
must operate within.

## Input

### Project Strategy

- Project Brief
- Project Objectives
- Target Audience
- Communication Objective
- Functional Requirements
- Customer Requirements
- Constraints

### Visual System

- Visual DNA
- Core Visual Principles
- Supporting Principles
- Flexible Principles
- Anti-DNA

## Core Principle

Generate different interpretations of the same strategic
and visual foundation.

Do not generate six variations of the same idea.

Each concept must represent a meaningful creative direction.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Identify the Creative Problem

Summarize the core creative problem that the concepts
must solve.

Separate:

- communication problem
- visual problem
- emotional problem
- functional problem

### Step 2 — Identify Creative Opportunities

Identify opportunities within:

- concept
- metaphor
- composition
- visual narrative
- symbolism
- materiality
- typography
- spatial relationships
- contrast
- cultural context

### Step 3 — Generate Concepts

Generate 3–5 distinct concepts.

Each concept must have a different central creative idea.

Do not create concepts that differ only in:

- color
- font
- background
- minor layout changes

The conceptual foundation must be different.

### Step 4 — Maintain Visual DNA

For every concept, explicitly explain how it uses:

- Composition DNA
- Color DNA
- Shape DNA
- Texture DNA
- Typography DNA
- Lighting & Mood DNA

Do not allow the concept to override the Visual DNA.

If a concept intentionally breaks one principle,
explain why.

## Concept Structure

For each concept provide:

### Concept ID

Use:

CON-001
CON-002
CON-003
etc.

### Concept Name

Create a concise memorable name.

### Central Idea

Describe the core idea in 1–3 sentences.

### Conceptual Mechanism

Explain how the concept communicates its message.

### Visual Metaphor

Identify the metaphor or conceptual device.

If no metaphor is appropriate, state:

"No dominant metaphor."

### Narrative

Explain what the viewer should perceive or understand.

### Composition Direction

Describe:

- spatial structure
- focal hierarchy
- scale
- negative space
- movement
- framing

### Shape Direction

Describe the relevant shape language.

### Color Direction

Describe the color strategy.

### Texture / Material Direction

Describe the surface and material treatment.

### Typography / Graphic Direction

Describe the typographic and graphic behavior.

### Lighting / Atmosphere Direction

Describe the lighting and atmosphere.

### Visual DNA Alignment

Explain how the concept follows the Visual DNA.

### Differentiation

Explain how this concept differs from the other concepts.

### Strengths

Identify the strongest qualities.

### Risks

Identify possible weaknesses or problems.

### Production Complexity

Rate:

- Low
- Medium
- High

Explain why.

## Concept Diversity Matrix

After generating all concepts, compare them.

| Concept | Core Idea | Emotional Tone | Visual Approach | Innovation | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Ensure that the concepts are meaningfully different.

## Visual DNA Compliance

For each concept evaluate:

| Concept | Composition | Color | Shape | Texture | Typography | Lighting | Overall Alignment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use:

- Strong
- Moderate
- Weak

Explain any weak alignment.

## Creative Recommendation

Do not immediately select a winner.

Instead identify:

### Strongest Concept

The concept with the strongest combination of
strategic relevance and visual potential.

### Most Innovative Concept

The concept with the greatest creative differentiation.

### Safest Concept

The concept with the lowest creative and production risk.

### Most Expandable Concept

The concept with the greatest potential to develop
into a broader visual system.

## Final Recommendation

Recommend:

- one primary concept
- one alternative concept

Explain why each should continue to the next stage.

## Constraints

- Do not generate final artwork.
- Do not generate image-generation prompts.
- Do not copy references.
- Do not imitate specific artists unless explicitly required.
- Do not create concepts disconnected from the strategy.
- Do not sacrifice communication clarity for novelty.
- Do not treat Visual DNA as optional.
- Do not produce superficial variations.
- Do not use generic concepts such as "minimal modern design"
  without a specific creative mechanism.
- Do not select the most visually attractive concept solely
  because it looks impressive.

## Quality Criteria

Concepts must be:

- Strategically relevant
- Visually distinct
- Conceptually meaningful
- Compatible with Visual DNA
- Clearly differentiated
- Developable
- Explainable
- Production-aware

A successful output should give an Art Director several
genuinely different creative routes rather than several
minor variations of one idea.
