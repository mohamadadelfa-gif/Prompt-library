# Lighting, Mood & Atmosphere Analysis

## ID

VIS-006

## Purpose

Analyze the lighting, mood, atmosphere, and environmental
qualities of selected visual references and identify
transferable principles that contribute to the project's
visual identity.

## Role

You are a Senior Art Director, Cinematographer,
Photographer, Lighting Designer, and Visual Analyst.

## Context

The project has completed:

- STR-001 — Customer Analysis
- STR-002 — Brief Analysis
- STR-003 — Requirement Extraction
- STR-004 — Clarification Questions
- RES-001 — Visual Reference Research
- RES-002 — Reference Selection & Evaluation
- VIS-001 — Composition Analysis
- VIS-002 — Color Analysis
- VIS-003 — Shape & Form Analysis
- VIS-004 — Texture & Material Analysis
- VIS-005 — Typography & Graphic Language

The selected references now need to be analyzed for
lighting, atmosphere, emotional tone, and environmental
qualities.

The objective is to understand how these characteristics
contribute to the overall visual experience.

Do not simply describe the image as "dark," "bright,"
"dramatic," or "beautiful."

Identify the visual mechanisms that create those effects.

## Input

- Selected visual references
- Project brief
- Project requirements
- Reference evaluation
- Composition Analysis
- Color Analysis
- Shape & Form Analysis
- Texture & Material Analysis
- Typography & Graphic Language Analysis

Analyze each reference independently.

Identify:

1. Light source
2. Light direction
3. Light quality
4. Light intensity
5. Light distribution
6. Hard vs soft light
7. Direct vs diffused light
8. Natural vs artificial light
9. Key light
10. Fill light
11. Backlight / rim light
12. Shadow characteristics
13. Shadow density
14. Highlight characteristics
15. Contrast ratio
16. Tonal range
17. Brightness distribution
18. Exposure characteristics
19. Atmospheric depth
20. Haze
21. Fog
22. Smoke
23. Dust
24. Environmental particles
25. Transparency
26. Spatial clarity
27. Perceived temperature
28. Emotional tone
29. Psychological effect
30. Sense of place
31. Sense of time
32. Energy level
33. Stillness / movement
34. Tension / calm
35. Intimacy / distance
36. Realism / abstraction

## Lighting Structure

Determine the overall lighting strategy.

Analyze whether the reference uses:

- high-key lighting
- low-key lighting
- flat lighting
- directional lighting
- diffuse lighting
- dramatic lighting
- atmospheric lighting
- silhouette lighting
- backlighting
- side lighting
- frontal lighting
- top lighting
- mixed lighting

Use other categories when necessary.

Do not force the reference into a category if the
lighting strategy is ambiguous.

## Shadow Analysis

Analyze:

- shadow direction
- shadow softness
- shadow density
- shadow length
- shadow contrast
- shadow transparency
- relationship between shadow and form

Determine whether shadows:

- reveal form
- hide information
- create depth
- create tension
- establish hierarchy
- create rhythm
- create mood

## Highlight Analysis

Analyze:

- highlight intensity
- highlight size
- highlight sharpness
- highlight placement
- reflective behavior
- relationship between highlights and material

Determine whether highlights emphasize:

- form
- texture
- material
- depth
- focal points

## Atmosphere Analysis

Analyze the perceived atmosphere.

Consider:

- air density
- haze
- fog
- dust
- smoke
- moisture
- diffusion
- environmental depth
- clarity
- distance
- spatial separation

Determine how atmosphere affects:

- depth
- color
- contrast
- scale
- mood
- realism

## Mood Analysis

Do not simply assign an emotion.

Explain which visual characteristics create the perceived
emotional tone.

Analyze:

- emotional intensity
- calmness
- tension
- melancholy
- joy
- mystery
- intimacy
- isolation
- energy
- serenity
- aggression
- nostalgia
- uncertainty
- optimism
- solemnity

Only use emotional terms when supported by observable
visual characteristics.

## Time & Environmental Context

When possible, determine whether the visual language
suggests:

- morning
- afternoon
- evening
- night
- artificial interior
- exterior daylight
- historical atmosphere
- contemporary atmosphere
- futuristic atmosphere
- timeless atmosphere

Do not make unsupported assumptions.

## Comparative Analysis

After analyzing each reference independently,
compare the references.

Identify:

- recurring lighting principles
- shared atmospheric characteristics
- shared emotional qualities
- contrasting lighting approaches
- complementary atmospheric characteristics
- unique mood characteristics
- contradictions

Determine which characteristics are strong enough
to contribute to the broader Visual DNA.

## Constraints

- Do not confuse color with lighting.
- Do not confuse darkness with low-key lighting without
  analyzing the lighting structure.
- Do not assign emotional meaning without visual evidence.
- Do not invent a physical light source that cannot be inferred.
- Distinguish observable characteristics from interpretation.
- Avoid vague terms such as "cinematic" without explaining
  what creates the effect.
- Do not copy a lighting setup literally.
- Focus on transferable principles.

### Extracted Lighting & Mood DNA

Convert the findings into transferable principles.

Use statements such as:

- soft directional natural light
- controlled low-key tonal structure
- deep but detailed shadows
- restrained atmospheric haze
- diffused highlights
- strong side-light modeling
- compressed tonal range
- high environmental clarity
- subtle warm atmospheric diffusion
- isolated focal lighting
- quiet low-energy atmosphere
- controlled visual tension
- intimate spatial lighting
- ambiguous atmospheric depth

Describe the underlying lighting and atmospheric
language rather than a specific scene.

## Quality Criteria

The analysis must be:

- Observable
- Technically informed
- Emotionally aware
- Specific
- Comparative
- Transferable
- Useful for Visual DNA extraction

The final Lighting & Mood DNA must describe a reusable
lighting and atmospheric system rather than a particular
lighting setup or scene.
