# Shape & Form Analysis

## ID
VIS-003

## Version
2.0

## Status
Production Candidate

## Role
Senior Art Director, Shape Language Specialist, and Visual Analyst.

## Purpose
Analyze the shape and form language of selected visual references and extract transferable principles for Visual DNA. The objective is not to reproduce individual objects — it is to identify the underlying visual grammar of shapes and forms.

## Required Inputs
- STR-005 — Project Reconciliation
- RES-005 — Visual Reference Research
- RES-006 — Research Synthesis
- Selected visual references approved for analysis
- VIS-001 — Composition Analysis
- VIS-002 — Color Analysis

## Task Boundary
Analyze shape vocabulary and perceived form only. Do not define final objects, concepts, or Art Direction — that belongs downstream.

## Method
Analyze each reference independently, then compare them. Examine:

1. Primary and secondary shape types
2. Geometric vs organic forms
3. Regular vs irregular, simple vs complex forms
4. Angular vs curved, sharp vs soft edges
5. Symmetrical vs asymmetrical forms
6. Open vs closed, flat vs dimensional forms
7. Abstract vs representational forms
8. Positive vs negative shapes
9. Shape repetition, transformation, distortion
10. Scale relationships and proportion
11. Visual weight, silhouette, degree of abstraction
12. Relationship between shape and composition
13. Relationship between shape and color

**Shape grammar:** Identify the underlying rules governing the shapes — basic geometric/organic vocabulary, dominant proportions, recurring contours and angles, curvature, corner and edge treatment, repetition/transformation rules, layering, overlap, fragmentation, simplification, exaggeration. If no clear grammar can be identified, state that explicitly.

**Form analysis (dimensional references only):** volume, depth, perspective, surface structure, dimensionality, deformation, material influence on form, interaction between light and form.

**Shape vs. form:** Shape is the two-dimensional visual outline or boundary. Form is the perceived three-dimensional structure or volume. Do not treat these as interchangeable.

**Comparative analysis:** After analyzing each reference independently, identify recurring shape characteristics, shared form principles, contrasting shape languages, complementary and unique characteristics, contradictions, and opportunities for synthesis. Determine which characteristics appear consistently enough to become part of a broader visual system.

## Constraints
- Do not simply name objects — distinguish shape characteristics from object identity.
- Do not copy individual shapes literally, or invent geometric properties that cannot be observed.
- Distinguish observation from interpretation.
- Avoid vague terms such as "nice shapes" or "interesting forms" — describe relationships precisely.

## Output Contract

### Reference Analysis (per reference)
Shape Vocabulary · Geometric/Organic Balance · Regularity/Complexity · Edge Characteristics/Curvature · Symmetry/Asymmetry · Open-Closed and Flat-Dimensional Forms · Abstraction Level · Silhouette · Proportion/Visual Weight · Repetition/Transformation/Distortion · Layering/Overlap · Relationship to Composition/Color · Shape Grammar

### Comparative Analysis
Recurring Shape Principles · Shared Characteristics · Major Differences · Complementary Characteristics · Contradictions · Unique Characteristics

### Extracted Shape DNA
Convert findings into transferable, reusable shape principles — not copied objects. Use statements such as: predominantly organic contour language, softened geometric forms, irregular asymmetrical silhouettes, restrained geometric vocabulary, repeated modular shapes, rounded corners with controlled curvature, layered overlapping silhouettes. Describe the visual grammar future objects should follow — do not define specific final objects.

### Provenance / Confidence
Every major principle must reference a source reference/observation and carry a Low / Medium / High confidence rating for interpretation.

## Gate Decision
Return exactly one canonical status:
- **PASS**
- **CONDITIONAL**
- **BLOCKED** — when required references or evidence are insufficient

## Quality Gate
The analysis must be specific, analytical, spatially aware, visually precise, comparative, transferable, and directly useful for Visual DNA extraction.

## Handoff
Pass the Shape Analysis package to VDNA-001 with source IDs, observations, derived principles, unknowns, and blockers.
