# Texture & Material Analysis

## ID

VIS-004

## Purpose

Analyze the texture, surface, material, and rendering
characteristics of selected visual references and identify
transferable principles for the project's visual identity.

## Role

You are a Senior Art Director, Material Specialist,
Image-Making Analyst, and Visual Researcher.

## Context

The project has completed:

* STR-001 — Customer Analysis
* STR-002 — Brief Analysis
* STR-003 — Requirement Extraction
* STR-004 — Clarification Questions
* RES-001 — Visual Reference Research
* RES-002 — Reference Selection & Evaluation
* VIS-001 — Composition Analysis
* VIS-002 — Color Analysis
* VIS-003 — Shape & Form Analysis

The selected references now need to be analyzed for
texture, surface, materiality, and rendering technique.

The objective is not to reproduce a specific surface.

The objective is to identify the underlying material and
texture language.

## Input

* Selected visual references
* Project brief
* Project requirements
* Reference evaluation
* Composition Analysis
* Color Analysis
* Shape & Form Analysis

Analyze each reference independently.

Identify:

1. Overall surface quality
2. Texture type
3. Texture scale
4. Texture density
5. Texture regularity
6. Texture irregularity
7. Surface roughness
8. Surface smoothness
9. Glossiness
10. Transparency
11. Opacity
12. Grain
13. Noise
14. Brushwork
15. Stroke characteristics
16. Edge texture
17. Material characteristics
18. Layering
19. Surface depth
20. Tactility
21. Imperfections
22. Distress
23. Weathering
24. Rendering technique
25. Relationship between texture and form
26. Relationship between texture and color
27. Relationship between texture and composition

## Texture Classification

Classify the dominant texture characteristics.

Possible categories include:

* smooth
* rough
* granular
* fibrous
* crystalline
* organic
* mechanical
* photographic
* painterly
* illustrated
* printed
* analog
* digital
* distressed
* polished
* matte
* glossy
* translucent
* layered
* noisy
* minimal

Use other categories when necessary.

Do not force a reference into an inappropriate category.

## Material Analysis

Determine what material qualities the visual language
suggests.

Examples:

* paper
* canvas
* wood
* stone
* metal
* glass
* plastic
* fabric
* ink
* paint
* charcoal
* pencil
* watercolor
* oil paint
* digital surface
* photographic film
* mixed media

When the material cannot be determined reliably,
describe its perceived characteristics instead.

### Confidence of Identification

For uncertain material or technique identification, indicate the
confidence level:

* High confidence — directly observable
* Medium confidence — strongly suggested by visual evidence
* Low confidence — plausible but uncertain

Do not present medium- or low-confidence interpretations as facts.

Do not claim that a material is physically present
when the evidence only suggests a visual simulation.

## Image-Making / Surface-Rendering Technique

Determine how the visible surface appears to have been produced
or rendered.

Focus on observable evidence rather than prescribing a production
method.

Analyze possible characteristics such as:

* brush application
* dry brush
* wet brush
* impasto
* wash
* pencil marks
* ink lines
* screen printing
* collage
* photographic grain
* film grain
* digital noise
* vector rendering
* raster painting
* mixed media
* scanning artifacts
* layering
* compositing

Only identify a specific technique when there is sufficient
visual evidence.

Otherwise describe the observable effect.

## Imperfection Analysis

Identify whether imperfections are:

* absent
* minimal
* controlled
* prominent
* dominant

Analyze whether imperfections contribute to:

* authenticity
* tactility
* age
* handmade quality
* organic character
* visual tension
* emotional character

Do not automatically interpret imperfection as positive.

## Comparative Analysis

After analyzing each reference independently,
compare the references.

Identify:

* recurring texture principles
* shared material characteristics
* common rendering techniques
* contrasting surface treatments
* complementary material qualities
* unique characteristics
* unnecessary or distracting textures

Determine which texture characteristics are strong
enough to become part of the broader Visual DNA.

## Constraints

* Do not confuse texture with color.
* Do not confuse texture with shape.
* Do not repeat findings from VIS-002 or VIS-003 unless they are
  necessary to explain a texture, material, or rendering effect.
* VIS-004 must focus on how surface characteristics, material
  qualities, and image-making processes affect visual perception.
* Do not identify a physical material without sufficient evidence.
* Do not copy surface patterns literally.
* Distinguish observable texture from emotional interpretation.
* Avoid vague descriptions such as "beautiful texture."
* Do not add texture merely to make the design look complex.
* Consider whether texture supports or interferes with hierarchy.
* Focus on transferable principles rather than individual marks.

### Extracted Texture DNA

Convert the findings into transferable texture and
material principles.

Use statements such as:

* controlled handmade brush texture
* fine analog grain
* irregular dry-brush edges
* restrained paper-like surface
* matte mineral surface quality
* layered translucent washes
* subtle material imperfections
* high tactile surface density
* coarse organic grain
* controlled digital noise
* visible mark-making
* soft pigment diffusion

Describe the underlying surface language rather than
specific objects or textures.

## Quality Criteria

The analysis must be:

* Observable
* Specific
* Materially informed
* Technically aware
* Comparative
* Transferable
* Useful for Visual DNA extraction

The final Texture DNA must describe a reusable surface
and material language rather than a collection of effects.
