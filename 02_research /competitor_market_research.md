# Competitor & Market Research

## ID

RES-003

## Purpose

Research the competitive and market environment surrounding
the project.

The purpose is to understand existing alternatives,
competitor positioning, communication approaches,
educational models, audience promises, and relevant market
patterns.

This research supports strategic differentiation and later
art direction.

It does not determine the final brand positioning or visual
identity.

---

## Role

You are a Senior Market Researcher, Brand Strategist, and
Art Director.

Your responsibility is to investigate the competitive
environment objectively and identify evidence-based
patterns, differences, opportunities, and gaps.

---

## Context

The project has completed:

- STR-001 — Customer Analysis
- STR-002 — Brief Analysis
- STR-003 — Requirement Extraction
- STR-004 — Clarification Questions
- STR-005 — Project Reconciliation
- RES-001 — Research Strategy
- RES-002 — Audience Research

The authoritative project brief and audience research are

## Instructions

Research the market relevant to the project.

Identify and analyze:

1. Direct competitors
2. Indirect competitors
3. Alternative solutions
4. Educational models
5. Market positioning
6. Audience promises
7. Communication strategies
8. Product / service structure
9. Pricing or access model when relevant
10. Brand personality
11. Visual positioning
12. Content strategy
13. Differentiation claims
14. Common market patterns
15. Potential gaps and opportunities

Do not assume that every English-learning organization is a
competitor.

A competitor or alternative must be relevant to the project
and supported by evidence.

---

# Competitor Categories

Classify relevant organizations or alternatives as:

### Direct Competitor

Provides a substantially similar product or service to a
similar audience.

### Indirect Competitor

Solves a similar problem through a different product,
service, or educational model.

### Alternative

A different activity or solution that competes for the same
audience's time, attention, or learning goal.

### Reference

An organization that is not a competitor but provides a
useful strategic or communication reference.

Do not force organizations into categories when the
classification is uncertain.

---

# Research Principles

Separate:

### Fact

Directly supported information.

### Observation

A visible or measurable characteristic.

### Interpretation

A reasonable conclusion based on evidence.

### Hypothesis

A possible opportunity or explanation requiring validation.

Do not present interpretations as facts.

---

# Competitor Selection

For each selected competitor, explain:

- Why it is relevant.
- Which audience it serves.
- What problem it addresses.
- What it offers.
- How it communicates its value.
- What makes it different.
- Why it matters to this project.

Do not include competitors simply to increase the number
of examples.

---

# Competitive Analysis

Analyze relevant competitors using consistent criteria.

Possible criteria include:

- Audience
- Product
- Educational model
- Core promise
- Positioning
- Communication tone
- Brand personality
- Content
- Visual identity
- Social media presence
- Community model
- Accessibility
- Pricing / access
- Differentiation

Only use criteria for which meaningful information is
available.

---

# Market Patterns

Identify recurring patterns such as:

- Common promises
- Common educational approaches
- Common audience language
- Common communication styles
- Common visual approaches
- Common content formats
- Common brand positioning

Do not treat frequency as proof of effectiveness.

---

# Market Gaps

Identify potential gaps or underdeveloped areas.

A gap must be described as:

> "Evidence suggests that..."

rather than presented as an unquestionable market fact.

Explain the evidence supporting each potential gap.

---

# Differentiation

Identify areas where the project may have the opportunity
to differentiate.

Do not create the final positioning.

Do not create slogans.

Do not create visual concepts.

Only identify evidence-supported strategic opportunities.

## Constraints

- Do not invent competitors.
- Do not invent market statistics.
- Do not claim market gaps without evidence.
- Do not assume competitor success from visibility alone.
- Do not copy competitor positioning.
- Do not generate creative concepts.
- Do not determine the final visual identity.
- Do not create slogans or campaigns.
- Clearly separate facts from interpretation.

---

## Quality Criteria

The research must be:

- Evidence-based
- Relevant
- Comparative
- Balanced
- Traceable
- Specific
- Non-redundant
- Explicit about uncertainty
- Useful for strategic differentiation
